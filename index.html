Why FBN?

Clubs often buy the same things separately.

Football clubs repeatedly purchase equipment, first aid, printing, teamwear, waste services, telecoms and other essentials. Yet many clubs source and negotiate these requirements independently.

FBN is testing whether bringing compatible demand together can give clubs stronger buying power, clearer price comparisons and less supplier admin — without taking control away from the club.

How it works

A simple process built around club choice.

01

Understand the club

We start with a short conversation about recurring costs, upcoming purchases and renewals. Clubs do not need to prepare a full procurement pack.

02

Identify buying opportunities

Where several clubs need similar products or services, FBN can combine that demand and test whether better commercial terms are available.

03

Let suppliers compete

Suppliers can quote against real demand. The club sees the opportunity and chooses whether or not to proceed.

The 30-club pilot

We are testing the model before building the platform.

FBN is currently in validation. The aim is to work with a small group of clubs, understand real purchasing behaviour, identify genuine shared demand and measure whether the model produces worthwhile savings.

There is no cost for a club to take part in the pilot and no obligation to buy through FBN or change an existing supplier.

What participation looks like

  • A 10–15 minute initial conversation
  • Share broad purchasing needs and upcoming renewals
  • Choose what information you are comfortable providing
  • Review any relevant group-buying opportunity
  • Decide independently whether the offer works for your club

About FBN

Starting locally. Building from evidence.

Football Buying Network is being developed by Lee Holness in Kent. The starting question is straightforward: can football clubs save meaningful money and time when compatible purchasing demand is brought together?

The current focus is not on building a large software platform. It is on working directly with clubs and suppliers, proving what works and using real results to shape what FBN becomes next.

Common questions

Before you take part.

Does the club pay to join the pilot?

No. There is no cost to a club to take part in the validation pilot.

Do we have to change suppliers?

No. Clubs remain in control of their purchasing decisions. If an FBN opportunity is not right for your club, you do not have to proceed.

Do we need to share sensitive financial information?

No. The pilot focuses on purchasing categories, approximate spend, suppliers, renewal timing and buying needs. Clubs should not share bank details, card details, passwords, payroll data or other unnecessary sensitive information.

How will FBN make money?

The model being tested is designed to keep club participation free while FBN earns through transparent supplier commissions, referral arrangements or future optional services where appropriate.
